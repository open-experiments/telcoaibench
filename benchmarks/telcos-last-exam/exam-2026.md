## IM1 · IMS & Voice · expert · 10 pts

**VoNR bearer dimensioning with EVS and ROHC**

A VoNR service uses EVS at 13.2 kbit/s with 20 ms packetization over IPv6.
a) Compute the RTP payload size per packet and the on-the-wire IP packet size without header compression.
b) Compute the resulting IP-layer bit rate, and the rate after ROHC compresses the IPv6/UDP/RTP stack to ~4 bytes.
c) State the 5QI used for the voice media flow and how the GBR you would configure relates to (b).
d) Explain why the SIP/IMS signalling flow rides a different QoS flow, and which 5QI that is.

**Reference answer:**

a) 13.2 kbit/s x 0.020 s = 264 bits = **33 bytes** of EVS payload per packet (plus ~1 byte payload-format ToC, acceptable). Header stack IPv6 40 + UDP 8 + RTP 12 = **60 bytes**, so ~**93 bytes** per packet on the wire.
b) 93 B / 20 ms = 4650 B/s = **37.2 kbit/s** at the IP layer. With ROHC compressing the 60-byte stack to ~4 bytes: 37 B / 20 ms ≈ **14.8 kbit/s**.
c) Voice media uses **5QI 1** (GBR, conversational voice). Provisioning cannot equate codec rate with transport demand: GFBR practice carries margin for the real traffic profile (commonly ~38-49 kbit/s per direction for EVS 13.2, though exact values vary by implementation and policy), because ROHC gain is a radio-layer optimization below the IP service flow and cannot be assumed at admission.
d) SIP signalling is carried on the IMS signalling PDU-session flow with **5QI 5** (non-GBR, high priority): signalling must survive cell congestion that would throttle a GBR media flow, and its burst profile (large SIP messages, sporadic) does not fit a small constant GBR.

**Grading notes:** Must have: 33-byte payload arithmetic, 60-byte IPv6/UDP/RTP stack, ~37 kbit/s uncompressed and ~15 kbit/s ROHC rates (±1 kbit/s tolerance for the ToC byte), 5QI 1 for media, 5QI 5 for signalling, and the reasoning that codec rate != transport demand so provisioning must carry margin for the real profile with ROHC treated as a radio-layer optimization. Accept IPv4 variant (20-byte header, 73 B, 29.2 kbit/s) if declared, and any GFBR/MBR provisioning practice that reasons correctly about overhead and margin. Penalize: equating codec rate with bearer rate without overhead reasoning, or 5QI 2/5QI 7 for voice media.

## IM2 · IMS & Voice · expert · 8 pts

**IMS-AKA registration security internals**

Walk through the security establishment of an initial IMS registration over 5GS: the two REGISTER rounds, what the 401 challenge carries, how the IPsec security associations between UE and P-CSCF are derived and addressed (SAs, SPIs, protected ports), and which headers negotiate them. Then state what integrity and confidentiality protection applies to all subsequent SIP signalling, and name one thing that changes when the UE registers over untrusted non-3GPP access instead.

**Reference answer:**

First REGISTER (unprotected) carries Security-Client (IPsec-3GPP: algorithms, SPIs, protected client ports). S-CSCF fetches AVs from HSS/UDM and returns **401 Unauthorized** with RAND and AUTN (WWW-Authenticate, AKAv2); CK/IK from Milenage/TUAK become the IPsec keys. UE and P-CSCF establish **four ESP SAs** (two pairs: UE-client<->P-CSCF-server and reverse), addressed by the negotiated SPIs, between protected client/server ports from Security-Client/Security-Server. The second REGISTER runs over the new SAs with the RES in the Authorization header; P-CSCF adds Security-Verify echoing Security-Server to defeat bid-down. From then on **all SIP signalling runs inside ESP** with the negotiated integrity (HMAC-SHA-1-96 or AES-GMAC family) and optional encryption; Service-Route/P-Associated-URI complete registration. Over untrusted non-3GPP access, the UE first builds an **IKEv2/IPsec tunnel to the N3IWF** (EAP-AKA'), so IMS security runs inside that outer tunnel — and P-CSCF discovery/addressing shifts to the tunnel's inner IP.

**Grading notes:** Must have: two-round REGISTER, 401 with RAND/AUTN, CK/IK -> ESP SA derivation, SPI + protected-port negotiation via Security-Client/Server/Verify, all subsequent signalling in ESP, and the N3IWF/IKEv2 difference for non-3GPP. Accept TS 33.203/24.229 citations but do not require them; accept architecture-correct variants that do not enumerate every SA/port detail — the four-SA structure and protected-port concept suffice. Penalize: TLS-only answers, claiming NAS security replaces IMS-AKA, or omitting the bid-down protection role of Security-Verify.

## IM3 · IMS & Voice · expert · 8 pts

**EPS fallback failure modes and timers**

An operator with NR SA but VoNR not yet enabled uses EPS fallback for voice. Describe the call-setup sequence from the QoS-flow trigger to the first RTP packet, including the two RAN-level realizations and the core-side requirement that makes context transfer seamless. Then analyze three production failure modes: (1) fallback without N26, (2) TAU-induced setup delay, and (3) return-to-NR ping-pong — for each, the mechanism and the mitigation.

**Reference answer:**

Sequence: MO/MT call -> the network attempts dedicated voice QoS-flow establishment (**5QI 1**); NG-RAN determines that voice cannot be supported over NR (configuration/capability, incl. N26 interworking availability) and initiates EPS fallback — one standardized realization is rejecting the 5QI-1 flow setup with cause 'IMS voice EPS fallback triggered' — executing either **handover to LTE** (with N26: Ngap HO -> S1) or **RRC release with redirection** to LTE. With **N26**, AMF<->MME context transfer keeps the PDN/PDU session and IMS registration alive; the dedicated **QCI-1** bearer is then set up in EPS and SIP UPDATE/PRACK completes; first RTP flows on QCI 1.
(1) **No N26**: UE must attach in EPS with 'handover' indication and re-establish PDN connections — several seconds of extra setup and possible IMS re-registration; mitigation: deploy N26 or accept redirection-based fallback only with combined-attach optimizations.
(2) **TAU delay**: after blind redirection the UE performs TAU (and possibly authentication) before the voice bearer can be set up; mitigation: HO-based fallback, pre-provisioned EPS security context via N26, and skipping authentication at TAU.
(3) **Ping-pong**: after call release the UE returns to NR (fast return via release-with-redirect to NR), then the next call falls back again; repeated inter-RAT transitions degrade data and battery. Mitigation: fast-return policies with hysteresis, keeping the UE in LTE during short inter-call gaps, or enabling VoNR on the loaded band first.

**Grading notes:** Must have: the sequence 'voice establishment -> NG-RAN determines NR voice unsupported -> fallback' (the literal 5QI-1-rejection cause is one valid realization, not required phrasing), both HO-based and redirection-based realizations, N26 context-transfer role, QCI-1 setup in EPS, and mechanism+mitigation for all three failure modes. Accept answers citing measured setup-time deltas (HO-based ~1-2 s faster than redirection+TAU). Penalize: claiming EPS fallback needs dual registration, or confusing EPS fallback with SRVCC (mid-call vs setup-time).

## IM4 · IMS & Voice · expert · 8 pts

**IMS emergency services under regulatory constraints**

Design the IMS emergency-call path for a 5G SA network in a jurisdiction requiring location-based PSAP routing and callback. Cover: how an unauthenticated/SIM-less UE is admitted, which dedicated IMS elements route the call and obtain location, how the network gets the position fix (name two positioning methods and the 5GC function that computes them), and what enables PSAP callback to a SIM-less caller. Note one architectural difference for roamers.

**Reference answer:**

The UE performs **emergency registration** (or anonymous/SIM-less access where mandated): AMF admits emergency service with **null security or limited service state**, and an **emergency PDU session** to a dedicated emergency DNN is established (subject to local regulation). The P-CSCF marks the session; the **E-CSCF** routes the call, querying the **LRF** (location retrieval function) for routing location; the **LMF** computes position over NL1 using e.g. **DL-TDOA/multi-RTT, A-GNSS, or NR E-CID** (TS 38.305 family), with control via AMF (Namf_Location). Routing uses the location to select the PSAP (via LRF/RDF). **Callback**: the PSAP calls a temporary routable identity bound by the LRF/E-CSCF to the emergency registration (reference identity / ESQK-style correlation), valid for a regulated time window — this is what works even for SIM-less callers as long as the emergency registration persists. Roamers: emergency calls **break out locally in the visited network** (no home routing) — the visited P/E-CSCF handles it, since PSAP selection and location are inherently local.

**Grading notes:** Must have: emergency registration incl. SIM-less admission, E-CSCF + LRF roles, LMF as the position-computing function with two concrete methods, temporary callback identity correlation, local breakout for roamers. Accept jurisdiction-specific registration, anonymous-access and callback realizations as fully valid when architecturally coherent — the reference is one realization, not the only one. Penalize: routing emergency via S-CSCF/home network, or claiming GPS-only positioning satisfies indoor mandates.

## OS2 · OSS/BSS · expert · 8 pts

**5G converged charging architecture**

Contrast 4G online/offline charging with 5G converged charging: name the 5G function and service-based interface involved, explain quota management for an online-charged data session (grant, thresholds, re-authorization), how offline records are produced in the same architecture, and where rating happens. Then explain what a charging architecture must add to support network-slice-differentiated pricing and 5QI-level rating without doubling CDR volume.

**Reference answer:**

4G split OCS (Gy/Ro, online quota) from OFCS (Rf/Ga, offline CDRs). 5G converges both in the **CHF** via **Nchf_ConvergedCharging** (SBI): the SMF acts as CTF, requesting **quota grants** per rating group (Create/Update), consuming against **volume/time/validity thresholds**, and re-authorizing on threshold hit, QoS change, or tariff-time change; the same session's usage reports feed **CHF-generated CDRs** (CGF integrated or separate) — one architecture, two outputs. **Rating** sits with the CHF/rating function (online: before/at grant; offline: at record processing). For slice/5QI-differentiated pricing: usage must be reported per **rating group x slice (S-NSSAI) x QoS level** — the PCF installs charging keys per PCC rule so the SMF reports at that granularity; the CHF then rates by (rating-group, S-NSSAI, 5QI) tuple. To avoid CDR explosion, aggregate at the charging-key level with slice/QoS as attributes of one record rather than separate CDR streams, and use change-triggers (QoS change, slice change) to cut records only at pricing-relevant boundaries.

**Grading notes:** Must have: CHF + Nchf_ConvergedCharging, SMF as the reporting CTF, quota grant/threshold/re-auth cycle, converged online+offline outputs, rating location, and per-(rating group, S-NSSAI, 5QI) reporting driven by PCC charging keys with aggregation to bound CDR volume. Accept Gy/Ro/Rf legacy names and CCR/CCA analogies. Penalize: placing quota logic in the UPF, claiming 5G has no offline charging, or per-packet rating proposals.

## OS3 · OSS/BSS · expert · 8 pts

**Inventory truth and automation safety**

An operator's automation increasingly executes changes computed from OSS inventory, but discovered network state and planned inventory disagree for ~8% of resources. Analyze: (a) three root causes of planned-vs-discovered drift, (b) the failure mode when closed-loop automation acts on stale inventory (give one concrete telco example), and (c) a reconciliation architecture — sources of truth per data class, discovery cadence, and how automation should gate on data quality. Include the role a network digital twin can and cannot play.

**Reference answer:**

(a) Root causes: manual field changes never written back (swap-outs, re-cabling); order fallout — activation partially executed but inventory updated optimistically; parallel writers (legacy OSS, NMS/EMS scripts, GitOps pipelines) without a single write path; plus naming/model mismatches across systems.
(b) Failure mode: automation computes a change against a resource that is not in the assumed state — e.g., a TN latency-rerouting loop moves a slice onto a link that inventory shows as 10GE spare but is actually carrying protection traffic after an un-synced field fix -> congestion/SLA breach; or RAN parameter push to a cell that was re-homed to a different DU.
(c) Reconciliation: declare **per-class sources of truth** — network-discovered state is authoritative for operational/config state (cadence: streaming/near-real-time for config, hourly for topology), planned inventory authoritative for intent/design and assignments; run continuous diff with materialized drift scores; auto-heal trivial classes (write-back), queue human review for conflicting classes. Automation gates: every closed-loop action validates a **freshness/consistency SLO** on the data it reads (e.g., 'topology reconciled < 1 h, drift score < threshold') and otherwise degrades to recommend-only. A digital twin **can** provide a consistent, versioned merge of discovered+planned state for what-if validation before execution; it **cannot** create truth — with a broken write-back discipline the twin drifts exactly like the inventory it mirrors.

**Grading notes:** Must have: >=3 plausible root causes, a concrete acted-on-stale-data failure example, per-data-class source-of-truth split (discovered = operational truth, planned = intent), continuous reconciliation with drift measurement, automation gating on data quality, and the twin's limits. Accept different cadence numbers. Penalize: 'make one system the master for everything', or twins presented as a fix for reconciliation.

## RG1 · Regulatory Services · expert · 10 pts

**Lawful interception in 5GC**

Describe the 3GPP lawful-interception architecture for a 5G SA network: the administrative and mediation functions, where interception points sit for (a) signalling events and (b) content of communication, and the three standardized interface families between them. Then explain two 5G-specific complications — one caused by SUPI concealment and one by home-routed roaming — and how the architecture addresses each.

**Reference answer:**

Per TS 33.126/127/128: warrants enter via the **ADMF complex** — **LICF** (control/legal policy) with **LIPF** provisioning the network; intercepted product flows to the LEMF through **MDF2** (intercept-related information, IRI) and **MDF3** (content, CC). **POIs**: (a) IRI-POIs embedded in control-plane NFs — AMF (registration/mobility events), SMF (session events), UDM (identity/subscriber events), SMSF, etc.; (b) CC-POI at the **UPF** (user-plane content, triggered via SMF/CC-TF). Interface families: **LI_X1** (provisioning/administration to POIs/MDFs), **LI_X2** (IRI from POI to MDF2), **LI_X3** (CC from POI to MDF3); delivery to the agency over LI_HI1/2/3 handover interfaces.
Complications: **SUCI concealment** — the target identifier on the air/N1 is an encrypted SUCI, so targeting by SUPI/IMSI requires the **UDM/SIDF IRI-POI** where de-concealment happens; the architecture mandates interception capability at the point where SUPI is available and correlation of SUCI-based events to the SUPI-based warrant. **Home-routed roaming** — for an inbound roamer the visited network cannot decrypt home-routed user plane nor see home-side services; each jurisdiction intercepts what terminates in it (VPLMN intercepts at V-SMF/UPF for visited-anchored traffic and serves visited-country warrants; HPLMN serves its own), and encrypted-service limitations are handled at the jurisdictional boundary rather than by breaking the N32/N9 security.

**Grading notes:** Must have: LICF/LIPF/ADMF, MDF2/MDF3 split, IRI-POIs in AMF/SMF/UDM, CC-POI at UPF, X1/X2/X3 roles, UDM-based answer to SUCI concealment, and jurisdiction-split answer for home-routed roaming. Accept mentioning virtualization/POI-in-CNF trust issues as extra; accept POI placements differing in detail from the reference (LI realization varies by service and deployment) — grade architecture and interface roles, not exact per-NF placement. Penalize: intercepting 'at the gNB', claiming SEPP decryption for LI, or conflating HI and X interfaces.

## RG3 · Regulatory Services · expert · 8 pts

**Caller-ID trust: STIR/SHAKEN end-to-end**

Explain how STIR/SHAKEN establishes caller-ID trust for IP voice: what is signed, by whom, the three attestation levels and what each asserts, how verification works at the terminating side, and the governance chain that makes certificates trustworthy. Then analyze two structural gaps — international/transit traffic and non-IP legs — and one mechanism the industry uses to bridge each.

**Reference answer:**

The originating provider signs a **PASSporT** (JWT: orig/dest TNs, iat, attestation, origid) carried in the SIP **Identity** header, using its **STI certificate**. Attestation: **A** — provider knows the customer *and* their right to use the number; **B** — knows the customer, cannot vouch for the number; **C** — only knows where it received the call (gateway). Terminating side: **STI-VS** fetches the cert via the x5u URL, validates signature/freshness and the chain to an approved **STI-CA**; results feed robocall analytics and display (and per local policy, blocking). Governance: a national **STI-PA** (policy administrator) authorizes providers via SPC tokens; STI-CAs issue certs only against those tokens — that chain is what binds a signature to an accountable provider. Gaps: (1) **International/transit** — a foreign originator has no SPC token, so calls arrive unsigned and gateways can only C-attest, which conveys near-zero trust; bridged by cross-border trust frameworks and **gateway providers taking responsibility** (know-your-upstream obligations, traceback participation). (2) **Non-IP (TDM) legs** strip SIP Identity; bridged by **out-of-band PASSporT delivery** (transmitting the token via a parallel service/CPS so the terminating side can still verify).

**Grading notes:** Must have: PASSporT-in-Identity signing, A/B/C attestation semantics, terminating verification against STI-CA chain, STI-PA/SPC-token governance, and both gaps with their bridges (cross-border/KYC-gateway; out-of-band delivery for TDM). Accept 'div' PASSporT for diversion as extra. Penalize: claiming SHAKEN blocks spoofing outright, verification 'by the PSAP', or ignoring that C-attestation carries little assurance.

## AI1 · AI & Autonomous Ops · expert · 10 pts

**NWDAF-driven predictive scaling with lead-time math**

Design an NWDAF-driven closed loop that scales a UPF deployment ahead of load. Given: AnLF publishes a load forecast every 5 min with a 15-min horizon and a quantified confidence; scaling out one UPF replica takes 3 min (schedule+start) plus 2 min soak before it takes traffic; scale-in is instant but re-anchors sessions.
a) Which two standardized NWDAF analytics IDs (by function, names acceptable) feed this loop, and which NWDAF split produces vs trains the model?
b) Derive the trigger rule: at what forecast condition must scale-out fire so capacity is ready before a predicted breach of 80% target utilization, and what forecast property (beyond the point estimate) must the rule consume?
c) Specify the anti-flapping design for scale-in given the session re-anchoring cost.
d) Name the failure mode if the forecast model is trained on data that already contains the loop's own scaling actions, and one way to break that circularity.

**Reference answer:**

a) **NF load analytics** (Nnwdaf 'NF load information') and **network performance / user-plane congestion analytics** feed the loop; the **AnLF** performs inference and exposes analytics, the **MTLF** trains and provisions the models (Nnwdaf_MLModelProvision).
b) Total lead need = 3 + 2 = **5 min**. The rule must fire when the forecast at lead time >= 5 min predicts crossing: trigger when the **upper confidence bound** (e.g., P90) of predicted utilization at t+5..t+15 min exceeds 80% — consuming the forecast's **uncertainty/confidence interval**, not the mean; with a 5-min publication period the effective decision latency adds up to 5 min, so in practice the rule evaluates the t+10 window to preserve margin.
c) Scale-in: **asymmetric thresholds with dwell** — e.g., only when P90 utilization < 60% continuously for >= 20 min (multiple forecast cycles), scale in one replica at a time with graceful **session draining** (quiesce new-session assignment via SMF UPF-selection weights, let sessions age out or re-anchor in batches), and enforce a cool-down that forbids scale-in within N min after any scale-out.
d) **Feedback contamination**: the model learns load patterns that embed the controller's own mitigations, so predictions of 'no breach' are conditional on actions the rule may then not take — the loop can unlearn the very signal it acts on. Break it by logging actions and training on **counterfactual-adjusted features** (include replica count/actions as model inputs), or training on control-free shadow periods / holdout cells.

**Grading notes:** Must have: AnLF/MTLF split, load-type analytics named, lead-time arithmetic (5 min) driving a trigger on an upper confidence bound (not the mean), asymmetric-with-dwell scale-in incl. draining, and the feedback-contamination failure mode with a mitigation. Accept different threshold numbers if internally consistent. Penalize: reactive-only designs, triggering on point estimates, or ignoring the publication-period latency entirely.

## AI2 · AI & Autonomous Ops · expert · 8 pts

**Model drift management for RAN AI**

A deployed RAN traffic-prediction model (feeds energy-saving cell shutdown) degrades after a stadium opens nearby. Define: (a) the two distinct drift types involved and a statistical detector for each; (b) a champion-challenger deployment design that lets a retrained model prove itself without risking KPIs; (c) the rollback trigger, stated as a measurable rule; and (d) the validation error that makes offline retraining results look better than they will be in production, with the guard against it.

**Reference answer:**

(a) **Data/covariate drift** — input distribution shifts (new event-day traffic peaks): detect with distribution-distance monitors per feature, e.g., **PSI or KL divergence** against the training reference window with alert thresholds (PSI > 0.2 conventionally). **Concept drift** — the input->target relationship changed (same features, new demand law): detect by monitoring **live prediction error** (rolling MAE/quantile loss) against the error budget, since input monitoring alone won't see it.
(b) Champion-challenger: challenger runs in **shadow mode** on live features producing predictions that are logged but not acted on; compare error over a window covering event and non-event days; then a limited **canary** on a low-risk cell cluster with automatic KPI guardrails (accessibility/retainability/energy KPIs) before fleet promotion.
(c) Rollback rule example: 'if rolling 24-h MAE of the active model exceeds the champion's baseline by >x% for 2 consecutive windows, OR any guarded KPI degrades beyond its threshold with shutdown actions active — revert to previous model version and freeze shutdown automation to conservative schedule.' Versioned model registry makes the revert one step.
(d) **Temporal leakage** (random train/test splits mixing future into past, or evaluation windows overlapping the drift event) — offline metrics look excellent because the model effectively saw the future. Guard: strict **chronological splits** with out-of-time test windows, and evaluating on post-change data only.

**Grading notes:** Must have: covariate-vs-concept drift distinction with a detector each (PSI/KL for inputs, live-error monitoring for concept), shadow mode -> canary with KPI guardrails, a concrete measurable rollback rule, temporal leakage named (chronological split as the guard). Accept ADWIN/DDM-style detectors. Penalize: 'retrain periodically' with no detection, validating on random splits, or acting on challenger predictions during shadow phase.

## AI3 · AI & Autonomous Ops · expert · 8 pts

**Intent conflict resolution per TS 28.312**

An energy-saving intent ('reduce RAN energy 20% in region X') and an assurance intent ('uplink latency P95 <= 20 ms for slice S in region X') decompose onto the same cells and collide: the energy MnS wants night shutdown of a capacity layer that slice S traffic uses. Describe how an intent-driven management system per TS 28.312 should handle this: the intent lifecycle stages where conflict can be caught, the difference between detection at decomposition time vs at runtime, and three resolution mechanisms an intent management function can apply. State what must be reported back to each intent owner.

**Reference answer:**

## TX5 · Transport & Fronthaul · expert · 8 pts

**SRv6 for slice-aware transport**

An operator builds SRv6-based metro transport for 5G slices. Explain: (a) how SRv6 expresses a low-latency slice path (mechanism, not marketing — what goes in the packet, what the ingress computes) and the role of Flex-Algo as the scalable alternative to explicit SID lists; (b) local protection — what TI-LFA provides and its guarantee; (c) two distinct ways slice separation can be realized in SRv6 transport with their isolation/scale trade-off; (d) the operational argument for SRv6 over RSVP-TE in this context (state two).

**Reference answer:**

(a) The ingress PE encapsulates traffic with an outer IPv6 header (+SRH when needed) whose destination/segment list steers the packet through **SIDs** — instructions (node/adjacency/service) computed by the ingress (or a PCE/controller) against a latency-optimized objective from the TE database (delay-measured links). **Flex-Algo** avoids per-flow SID stacks: the IGP computes an additional topology algorithm (e.g., algo 128 = 'minimize measured delay, exclude non-encrypted links'), each participating node gets an algo-specific SID, and one SID steers the whole path along the delay-optimal tree — per-destination low-latency forwarding with near-zero header overhead and no per-path state.
(b) **TI-LFA** precomputes per-destination backup paths (using SRv6 repair SIDs to tunnel around the failure to the post-convergence path), giving **~50 ms-class local repair** upon link/node failure detection without waiting for IGP convergence, and — its distinguishing property — the backup follows the **post-convergence path** (no transient loops/suboptimal detours).
(c) Slice separation options: (1) **logical**: slice-per-Flex-Algo/SR policy with H-QoS per slice on shared links — scales well (state in IGP/policies, not per-flow), isolation is statistical (scheduling), no capacity hard split; (2) **hard**: slice-per-dedicated-resource — FlexE channels / dedicated lambdas / separate physical or virtual sub-interfaces mapped to SR policies — deterministic isolation (one slice's burst cannot touch another's capacity) at the cost of stranded capacity and coarser granularity. Choice follows what the slice SLA sold.
(d) SRv6 vs RSVP-TE: (1) **no per-LSP soft state in the core** — RSVP-TE holds per-tunnel state on every transit node (refresh, scaling limits, full-mesh O(N^2) problems); SR keeps state only at the ingress/controller; (2) **native IPv6 data-plane simplicity and service programming** — one protocol family end-to-end (metro to core), SIDs can encode service functions (chains) and integrate with the 5G user plane, while protection (TI-LFA) and ECMP behave natively — operationally fewer protocols to run and troubleshoot.

**Grading notes:** Must have: SID/SRH steering with ingress/PCE computation, Flex-Algo as per-destination algo-SID scaling, TI-LFA 50 ms + post-convergence-path property, both separation modes with the statistical-vs-deterministic trade, two concrete RSVP-TE contrasts (state, protocol simplification). Accept uSID mention as bonus. Penalize: SRv6 described as 'MPLS with bigger labels' without state argument, or claiming logical slicing gives hard isolation.

Design the timing architecture: (a) what changes between G.8275.1 and G.8275.2 profiles and what APTS means operationally; (b) why leased dark-segment asymmetry is the dominant error source in partial support and how it is bounded; (c) a two-layer GNSS attack defense — detection signals and the holdover strategy with concrete class/duration expectations; (d) the network-side backstop that limits how long any site must free-run.

(a) **G.8275.1** = full timing support, every node a T-BC/T-TC, PTP over L2 hop-by-hop; **G.8275.2** = partial timing support, PTP over IP through PTP-unaware segments (unicast), tolerating nodes that add uncorrected delay. **APTS** (assisted partial timing support): GNSS at the edge site is the primary reference and the PTP path runs as *assist/backup* — while GNSS is healthy the site calibrates the PTP path's asymmetry against GNSS, so on GNSS loss the PTP backup takes over with a pre-measured correction.
(b) PTP measures one mean path delay and assumes symmetry; through unaware leased segments, **forward/reverse delay differ** (different fiber routes/lengths, congestion-dependent queuing per direction) and half the asymmetry lands directly as time error — µs-class if uncontrolled, dwarfing every other contributor. (d) Backstop: a **resilient distributed PRTC architecture** — regional ePRTC/cesium-anchored grandmasters (and increasingly, sources independent of open-sky GNSS such as fiber-delivered ePRTC references) feeding the PTP network, so a GNSS event at a site (or regionally) degrades to 'PTP from an unaffected anchor', bounding free-run to the switchover time rather than the attack duration; TDD neighbors sharing a sync island also bound *relative* error, which is what interference actually cares about.

**Grading notes:** Must have: 8275.1 vs .2 vs APTS operational distinction, asymmetry-as-dominant-error with calibration bounding, two-layer defense (integrity detection incl. spoofing-vs-jamming signatures; holdover classes with realistic durations), and network-anchor backstop with the relative-error insight. Oscillator holdover durations are illustrative — accept any figures consistent with the stated oscillator class and sound engineering; grade the strategy, not the constants. Penalize: 'GNSS everywhere' with no attack story, symmetric-path assumption unexamined, or holdover treated as unlimited.

## SE3 · Security · expert · 8 pts

**SBA authorization attack surface**

Within a 5G SBA core, NF-to-NF authorization uses OAuth2 with the NRF as authorization server. Analyze the trust model: (a) what a service-request access token asserts and the two layers beneath it that must also hold; (b) three concrete abuse scenarios if token scoping or validation is weak (name the attack pattern each maps to); (c) the hardening added when an SCP sits in the path (delegated discovery) and the client-credential binding that prevents a stolen token being replayed by another NF; (d) why 'the core is a trusted zone' failed as an assumption — give the operational reality that killed it.

**Reference answer:**

(a) The token (JWT signed by NRF) asserts: this consumer NF (instance/type) may invoke this producer NF type/service — audience, scope, expiry. Beneath it: (1) **mutual TLS** authenticating both endpoints (the token is only as good as the certificate identity it was issued to and presented with), and (2) **NF registration integrity at the NRF** — profiles/discovery must be authentic, else tokens get issued to/for imposters.
(b) Abuses: **over-broad scope** (token for Nudm-sdm read reused to hit other UDM services) -> privilege escalation / confused-deputy; **audience confusion** (token minted for producer A accepted by producer B due to lax audience validation) -> lateral movement; **rogue NF registration** (attacker registers a fake NF/updates a profile at a weakly-protected NRF, gets discovered and receives legitimate traffic or harvests tokens) -> man-in-the-middle/data harvesting at core scale (also: token replay from a compromised NF if tokens aren't sender-bound).
(c) With **SCP delegated discovery**, the SCP performs discovery/selection and forwards requests; hardening (Rel-17) adds **CCA — Client Credentials Assertion** — the consumer signs its own identity assertion that travels with the request so the producer/NRF can verify *which NF actually asked* even through intermediaries, binding token issuance/usage to the authenticated client rather than 'whoever presented it'; combined with per-hop mTLS and audience+scope enforcement at the producer, a stolen bearer token alone stops being sufficient.
(d) The trusted-zone assumption died with the operational reality of the **cloud-native, multi-vendor, multi-tenant core**: NFs are containers from many vendors on shared Kubernetes with dozens of admin/CI/CD paths, third-party integrations, exposed OAM planes, and workloads spanning sites/public cloud — a single compromised pod, image, or pipeline sits *inside* the old perimeter. Hence zero-trust posture on the SBI: authenticate and authorize every call, assume any peer can be compromised, and scope blast radius per service.

**Grading notes:** Must have: token semantics + mTLS and NRF-registration underpinnings, three concrete abuse scenarios with escalation/lateral/rogue-NF patterns, SCP context with CCA as client binding, and the cloud-native/multi-vendor argument ending the trusted-zone model. Accept mentions of token audience being NF-instance-scoped as strengthening. Penalize: 'TLS solves it', treating NRF as unconditionally trustworthy, or no stolen-token discussion.

## SE4 · Security · expert · 8 pts

**Quantum-safe migration for a mobile operator**

Plan the post-quantum migration for a mobile operator. (a) Triage which telco cryptographic assets are actually exposed to harvest-now-decrypt-later and which are not, with reasoning (cover: subscriber air-interface keys, SUCI concealment, N32/TLS interconnect, IPsec backhaul, code signing). (b) State the migration mechanism available today for the transport layers — including the current standardization-status split between TLS 1.3 and IKEv2 — and why 'hybrid' is the recommended mode. (c) Explain the crypto-agility requirements this forces on procurement. (d) Name the pragmatic sequencing (what first, what last) with justification.

**Reference answer:**

(a) Triage by data lifetime + key exchange type: **HNDL-exposed** = anything whose *recorded ciphertext* stays sensitive for years and whose confidentiality rests on public-key exchange: **N32/SBA TLS and IPsec backhaul/roaming tunnels** (subscriber data, signalling, LI-adjacent traffic recorded today, decrypted when a CRQC arrives) and **SUCI concealment** (ECIES with X25519/P-256 — recorded SUCIs could be de-concealed later, a privacy exposure; the identity mapping stays sensitive indefinitely). **Less exposed**: **air-interface session keys** — derived via symmetric AKA hierarchies (128/256-bit symmetric, quantum impact only Grover-halving -> 256-bit symmetric is fine), and sessions are short-lived; **code signing / firmware** — not HNDL (integrity, not confidentiality) but must be PQ **before** CRQC since a forged signature is exploited *live*; long-lived device roots-of-trust deserve early attention (field lifetime 10-15 yr).
(b) Mechanism: **hybrid key exchange** — classical ECDH combined with **ML-KEM** so the session is safe unless *both* are broken. The status split matters: for **TLS 1.3**, hybrid groups are standardized and deployable now (e.g., X25519MLKEM768); for **IKEv2**, ML-KEM integration is far advanced in the IETF process (ipsecme work, implementable vendor-side/experimentally) but not yet an established RFC mechanism — an expert plan states this asymmetry rather than treating both as done. Hybrid is recommended because PQC algorithms/implementations are young (implementation bugs, potential cryptanalysis) — hybrid preserves today's security floor while adding quantum resistance.
(c) Crypto-agility in procurement: require **negotiable/upgradable cryptographic primitives** (no hard-coded algorithms), certificate/PKI tooling that handles larger PQ keys/signatures (ML-DSA sizes affect handshakes, MTU, HSMs), firmware-updatable crypto in long-lived field hardware, vendor roadmaps with dates for hybrid TLS/IKEv2 and PQ certificates, and inventory/SBOM-style **cryptographic bills of materials** so exposure is auditable.
(d) Sequencing: **first** the long-haul recorded-traffic transports (N32/roaming TLS, inter-site IPsec — highest HNDL exposure, fewest endpoints, hybrid available now) and new **device/firmware roots-of-trust** entering the field (irreversible 10-15 yr lifetime); **then** internal SBA/TLS and SUCI profile evolution (needs 3GPP-standardized PQ ECIES replacement, UE+UDM coordination); **last** air-interface mechanisms (already symmetric-safe) and short-lived internal credentials — driven by exposure window x replacement lead time, not by uniform deadlines.

**Grading notes:** Must have: HNDL triage separating recorded-transport + SUCI (exposed) from symmetric air-interface (largely safe) with signing as pre-CRQC-integrity case, hybrid ML-KEM with the TLS-1.3-standardized vs IKEv2-still-in-IETF-process status split and the immaturity rationale, concrete agility procurement asks, and lifetime-driven sequencing (interconnect + roots-of-trust first). Accept Grover/AES-256 framing. Penalize: 'replace everything at once', claiming AKA breaks under quantum, or omitting SUCI.

**Reference answer:**

b) Correct cycle per replica: (1) **quiesce** — SMF removes the replica from selection for new sessions (weight 0); (2) **drain** — re-anchor or age-out its 50k sessions: 50,000/500 = **100 s ≈ 1.7 min** of controlled re-anchoring (SSC-mode-aware: SSC2/3 make-before-break where subscribed; else scheduled re-establishment); (3) **upgrade + startup 2 min**; (4) **soak 10 min** with live canary traffic before it re-enters selection at full weight. c) **Externalized/replicated session state** (N4 session contexts + forwarding state in a shared store or state-sync to a hot standby, e.g., checkpointed session DB or 1+1 state replication): the new pod adopts sessions instead of draining them, cutting drain to seconds. d) Promotion gates: (1) **session/service KPIs on the upgraded replica during soak** — session establishment success, GTP-U throughput/packet loss, N4 heartbeat/error rates vs baseline; (2) **fleet-level SLA/KPI stability** — no accessibility/latency regression and no error-budget burn at the slice/service level while the canary carries traffic. Pod 'Ready' only proves the process started; promotion needs *service* evidence.

Accept different soak times with recomputed windows; SSC-mode nuance is bonus; accept state replication, hot standby, vendor session checkpointing, or control-plane-driven session reconstruction as equally valid alternatives to a shared external store. Penalize: relying on PDB/readiness alone, draining by killing sessions, or ignoring the surge-capacity requirement.
